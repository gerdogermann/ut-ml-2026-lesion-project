# Probe pretrained anatomy features before swapping encoders

Yes, a pretrained CT encoder can serve Team 2's objective, and nnFoundation is a real, usable candidate. Its convolutional half, **nnFoundationCNN (ResEnc-L, 102 M params, MAE-pretrained on 2.16 M CT/MRI/PET volumes)**, can replace the current encoder in SiameseNet without touching the correlation and soft-argmax head. But nnFoundation was built and benchmarked for segmentation, and no source tests it on lesion correspondence. The strongest published evidence that pretraining helps lesion *tracking* comes from a different family: self-supervised anatomical embeddings (SAM, SAM++). These use **no lesion labels yet beat the supervised Deep Lesion Tracker, 6.1 mm vs 7.0 mm mean error, with a smaller spread (σ 6.7 vs 8.9 mm)**. SAM also works natively at 2 mm, the project's spacing. Integration is practical: tap the 4 mm stage of a truncated ResEnc (about 4 M params by the researcher's own count), fix a half-voxel grid offset that the current code would silently absorb, map intensities, warm up at a lower backbone learning rate, and always pair each pretrained run with a random-init control. Expect modest gains concentrated in the gross-miss tail, not in the 3.49 mm median, which is already near the scale of centre-label ambiguity. On the third question: review the worst cases now on **validation**, not test. The test split has not been touched by any Team 2 model yet. Review its worst cases exactly once, after the final model and readout are frozen and the test number is logged, and report that review as post-hoc failure analysis.

## nnFoundation fits CT, but was built for segmentation

"nnFoundation" is a DKFZ model pair released in late 2026 in the `nnssl` repository: nnFoundationCNN, a 6-stage residual encoder with 32–64–128–256–320–320 channels, and nnFoundationViT, a 674 M-param Primus transformer with 8³-voxel tokens ([MIC-DKFZ/nnssl](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnssl); [HF nnFoundationCNN](https://huggingface.co/MIC-DKFZ/nnFoundationCNN)). Both were MAE-pretrained on **2,158,570 volumes from 125 sources: 71.7% MRI, 26.6% CT, 1.7% PET**. That is roughly 574 k CT volumes ([nnFoundation, arXiv 2609.26924](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26924v1)). The pretraining data was "not resampled to a fixed target spacing", and every image was z-score normalised. Weights are CC-BY-SA-4.0, so derived models must be shared alike and attributed ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/MIC-DKFZ/nnFoundationCNN)). The earlier OpenMind checkpoints from the same group were pretrained on 114 k MRI volumes at 1 mm, so they are out for a CT task ([OpenMind, arXiv 2412.17041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17041)).

The paper's headline numbers are all segmentation. Across 34 datasets, nnFoundationCNN beat CT-FM by **3.0 Dice** and a from-scratch nnU-Net by **1.9 Dice**. The gain was about +3 Dice with only 10 training cases. The authors write that "the convolutional model dominates spatially localized tasks, whereas the transformer model excels in tasks requiring global semantic reasoning" ([arXiv 2609.26924](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26924v1)). The researchers read only the first half of the paper and found no registration, correspondence or frozen-feature results there. Whether the second half has any is unknown. That sentence settles the choice between the two models: the CNN is the candidate. The ViT's 8³ tokens become 16 mm cells on 2 mm patches, so a 64³ crop yields only an 8³ token grid, far coarser than the current 4 mm correlation grid.

Other released CT encoders sort by how well their training objective matches dense BL-to-FU matching. The table below ranks them for this task. The ranking is the researchers' judgement; no source benchmarks these models on the project's patches.

## Anatomical embeddings, not MAE features, carry the tracking evidence

The DeepLesion tracking benchmark (DLS) is the closest public analogue to Team 2's task. Its results are unambiguous on one point. **SAM, trained without a single lesion label, reaches 6.1 ± 6.7 mm mean error and 86.0% CPM@10 mm. Supervised DLT, trained from scratch on about 3 k lesion pairs, reaches 7.0 ± 8.9 mm and 78.9%.** SAM++ improves this to 5.4 ± 6.0 mm and 88.3% ([SAM++, arXiv 2306.13988](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13988); [DLT, arXiv 2012.04872](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.04872v2)). The training regime is comparable to Team 2's 2,159 training lesions. The gain shows up mostly as a narrower error distribution, which is the part of Team 2's error that matters: the run 6b log notes that "the remaining mean comes from a few gross misses" ([Experiments.md](../docs/wiki/Experiments.md), row 11). SAM's own ablation shows where the gain comes from. Removing coarse-to-fine matching drops lesion-matching accuracy from 91.1% to 84.1%. A local embedding alone "can produce a large maximum error", while the coarse embedding "can provide a rough localization" ([SAM, arXiv 2012.02383](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.02383v3)). Gross misses on look-alike neighbours are exactly what coarse anatomical context removes.

Evidence for generic SSL encoders is weaker. On OpenMind's 12-dataset segmentation benchmark, the best SSL method (MAE) added only **+2.47 Dice** to a ResEnc-L. Several methods added nothing, and a from-scratch model trained 1,000 epochs caught up with most of them. Most relevant here: a **frozen MAE encoder scored 30.38 Dice against 68.36 for default fine-tuning** ([OpenMind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17041)). MAE features are not usable as-is for dense tasks; they need fine-tuning. On the project's own dataset, DKFZ's autoPET IV entry raised segmentation Dice from 55.78 from scratch to 62.27–62.89 with pretraining ([arXiv 2509.00613](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00613v1)). LesionLocator's synthetic longitudinal pretraining cut point-prompt centre error from 5.09 to 3.89 mm on 60 melanoma patients ([LesionLocator, arXiv 2502.20985](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20985)). Both are encouraging. Neither isolates what a pretrained encoder does inside a Siamese correlation tracker, and the researchers found no study that does.

Two cautions keep expectations realistic. First, Team 2's 5.58 mm sits between SAM (6.1) and SAM++ (5.4) on paper, but the comparison is unfair in Team 2's favour. DLS trackers search whole volumes or registration-initialised regions. Team 2's FU window is already centred within ±24 mm of the lesion, so far-away distractors never appear. The 12.47 mm zero baseline plays roughly the role of DLS's 11.2 mm affine baseline. Second, the median is close to a floor. On an earlier 206-pair version of the same Tübingen cohort, registration-propagated centres sat 3.79 / 3.16 / 4.49 mm (x/y/z) from the manual follow-up centres ([Hering et al., MIDL 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v143/hering21a/hering21a.pdf)). That figure measures registration error, not inter-reader noise. No published label-error rate exists for this dataset. Still, it shows that "the FU centre" is a fuzzy target at the few-mm scale. A pretrained encoder that also works at 2–4 mm resolution will not sharpen the median much. Its realistic payoff is fewer gross misses.

## A truncated nnFoundationCNN drops into SiameseNet with four fixes

The current model ([model.py](../src/lesionloc/model.py)) is a 2-channel stem (`Conv3d(k=4, s=2, p=1)`) followed by three dilated residual blocks on a 32³ grid of 4 mm cells. It produces 32-channel L2-normalised features, correlates a 5³ BL template with the FU map, and reads out a soft-argmax. Everything after the encoder can stay. With 2 mm input, **ResEnc stage 1 is the native 4 mm grid**: 64 channels, 32³, three residual blocks. Stage 2 is the 8 mm context grid: 128 channels, 16³. The stage strides `[1,2,2,2,2,2]` and channels come from the nnssl architecture registry ([architecture_registry.py](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnssl/blob/nnFoundation/src/nnssl/architectures/architecture_registry.py)). Stages 3–5, which hold most of the 102 M parameters, are discarded. By the researcher's own arithmetic, stem through stage 2 is **about 4.3 M parameters**, roughly 35× the current 0.12 M. Do not build a U-Net decoder: pretrained decoders are not transferred in nnFoundation or OpenMind, so it would start random ([arXiv 2609.26924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26924)). Instead, project each tapped stage with a 1×1 conv to 32 channels and L2-normalise it. Then correlate at both scales and **sum the two correlation maps** after upsampling the 8 mm one. This is how SAM combines its global and local embeddings (S^g + S^l) and how DLT fuses its scales ([SAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02383); [DLT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04872)). The researcher estimates that stage 1 alone sees about 60 mm, less than the current encoder's ~100 mm (code comment). That estimate is a second reason to add the 8 mm map.

The four fixes, each traced against the repo code:

**Grid offset.** The current stem centres cell *i* on voxel 2i + 0.5, and `cell_mm()` assumes this. ResEnc's stride-2 conv is k3/s2/p1, which centres cell *i* on voxel 2i. Reading the code confirms the researcher's arithmetic: every cell would sit **1 mm per axis (about 1.7 mm Euclidean) from where `coords` says it is**. This is a derived claim, not a measured bias. Flip TTA would mask it, because the offset changes sign under each flip and cancels in the 8-flip average. The plain readout would carry it. The fix is to subtract 1 mm from `coords` and move the BL template to cell 15.75 (weights 0.25/0.75 per axis, in place of the 2×2×2 mean). The mask pooling must also become `(3, 2, 1)` in two places: `model.py` and `losses.py`, whose mask term hard-codes `F.max_pool3d(fu_mask, 4, stride=2, padding=1)  # same grid as SiameseNet's stem`. Verify the fix with a synthetic delta at voxel 31.5 + 2j, which should read out as 4j mm.

**Intensity.** Patches store `(HU + 1000) / 1400`, clipped to [−1000, 400] HU ([preprocess.py](../src/lesionloc/preprocess.py)). nnFoundation expects z-scored input. ResEnc applies affine InstanceNorm after a linear stem conv, so any global map `a·x + b` (a > 0) is normalised away, except at zero-padded borders. The exact z-score constants therefore barely matter. Undo the scaling (`hu = x*1400 - 1000`) and apply a fixed z-score from a few training CTs. The irreducible mismatch is the 400 HU clip, because the encoder saw bone, contrast and metal unclipped. A wider window means re-cutting patches, so treat it as a later ablation.

**Mask channel.** The pretrained stem takes one channel; the current encoder takes `[CT, BL mask]`. Start with the CT-only stem and let the existing mask-pooled template (`--bl-mask`) carry the mask. Later, a zero-initialised second stem channel keeps the step-0 function identical to the pretrained one. Do not use nnU-Net's default of repeating the stem weights across channels: that suits a second image modality, not a binary mask. One code pitfall: the conv block calls `self.all_modules(x)`, and `.conv` is only an alias. A swapped conv must therefore be written into both places ([simple_conv_blocks.py](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/dynamic-network-architectures/blob/main/dynamic_network_architectures/building_blocks/simple_conv_blocks.py)).

**Optimiser.** `train.py` uses one AdamW group at 3e-4 with no warmup. Freeze the stem and stage 0. Fine-tune stages 1–2 at about 1e-4 and 5e-5. Keep the new head at 3e-4 and use a 10–15% linear warmup. This follows the warm-up schedules and reduced transfer LRs in OpenMind and nnFoundation ([OpenMind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17041); [nnFoundation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26924)). It also follows the finding that tuning intermediate layers beats tuning only the last ones ([arXiv 2307.10915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10915)). Keep InstanceNorm and EMA as they are.

Compute is not the constraint. All of the following are the researcher's estimates, not measurements. Training at batch 16 pairs needs roughly **2–4 GB with the stem and stage 0 frozen**, and 6–9 GB fully fine-tuned, in fp16 AMP (V100 has no bf16). Training is already data-loading bound at about 182 s per epoch ([Experiments.md](../docs/wiki/Experiments.md), row 10), so a slower forward pass may cost little wall time. Do not cache frozen features. Random 3-axis flips and odd-voxel FU jitter would need 64 variants per lesion. The researcher's estimate of one variant alone is ~117 GB for the training set. Measure the real cost with the existing `--bench` flag.

On spacing, the notes disagree. The integration note calls 2 mm close enough to the 1.5 mm most CT checkpoints saw. The landscape note points out that nnFoundation's unresampled pretraining data was mostly ~0.7–1 mm in-plane, so 2 mm patches double the effective receptive field in mm. Neither side has a measurement. Keep 2 mm for the first runs, because re-cutting at 1 mm costs about 8× the compute, and treat resolution as an ablation if the pretrained runs look promising.

Run the experiment as matched pairs. Only a same-architecture random-init control separates the effect of pretraining from the effect of a different, larger encoder. This is the same logic as nnU-Net's `--from_scratch` baseline for nnssl checkpoints ([nnU-Net doc](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnUNet/blob/master/documentation/finetuning_from_nnssl_checkpoints.md)).

| Run | Encoder | Trained | Question answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 | SAM / UAE-S frozen, BL-centre embedding argmax in FU patch | nothing | Do 2 mm anatomical embeddings localise these lesions zero-shot? Compare with 12.47 and 5.58 mm |
| P1 / P1-rand | nnFoundationCNN stem→stage 1 + 1×1 proj, frozen / random-init trainable | head / all | Do pretrained 4 mm features carry matching signal? |
| P3 / P3-rand | + stage-2 map, summed logits, stages 1–2 fine-tuned / random init | partial / all | Pretraining effect under fine-tuning (expected best) |
| P4 | P3 + zero-init BL-mask stem channel | partial+ | Mask in the encoder vs head only |

All P runs keep run 6b's flags (`--rejitter-sd 8 --bl-mask --lambda-mask 0.5 --aug-root … --ema 0.999 --epochs 50 --template-k 5`). Expect P1 to lose to 6b, given OpenMind's frozen-MAE result; a loss there is not a reason to stop. If S0 is strong, SAM becomes the better backbone, despite its obsolete mmdet stack: port its ResNet-18+FPN to plain PyTorch, or fuse its similarity map with the current one. Keep a pretrained encoder only if it beats both its random-init twin and 6b in a **paired, patient-clustered comparison** on the 207 val lesions. The log already treats gaps of about 0.05 mm on this set as ties ([Experiments.md](../docs/wiki/Experiments.md), rows 8–12); the researcher's proposed keep-threshold of ≥ 0.3 mm on the TTA mean is a rule of thumb, not a power analysis.

## Review validation worst cases now; open test only once

The test split is clean, and that is worth protecting. The experiment log shows test touched once, for the zero baseline (12.52 mm); every Team 2 model was selected on val ([Experiments.md](../docs/wiki/Experiments.md), row 2). The project rule is "Use the test split only for final numbers" ([Teams.md](../docs/wiki/Teams.md)). Holdout guarantees assume "a fixed procedure to be performed, selected before the data are examined". Once a model is modified based on holdout results, scores keep rising while true performance does not ([Dwork et al., Science 2015](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaa9375)). The danger is acute here. Test has 286 lesions from about 30 patients ([Gatidis et al., Sci Data 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07466-y)), and Team 2's cheapest knobs could easily be fitted to a few test patients: TTA on/off, local soft-argmax radius, prior σ, filters for MERGING or tiny lesions. So do not look at test worst cases now. Once the final model is frozen and its test number logged, failure analysis on test is legitimate and expected. CLAIM 2024 item 39 asks authors to present incorrectly handled cases "to help readers better understand the strengths and limitations of the algorithm" ([CLAIM 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11304031/)). If that review suggests a fix, evaluate it on val. Any re-scored test number must be labelled test-informed.

On val, a worst-case review is worth doing now, and it feeds directly into the encoder decision. The run 5 top 20 already showed a pattern ([Experiments.md](../docs/wiki/Experiments.md), row 8). Six came from patient e2c0be2456 and four from 3988c7f88e; 8 were tiny lesions (r < 5 mm) and 3 huge (r > 20 mm); every one had peak probability below 0.43. Those 20 cases are hypotheses, not findings. Error auditing finds candidate subgroups, but each must be confirmed as a stratum metric over the whole split ([Oakden-Rayner et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.12475v2)). In that study's own example, a pattern seen in false negatives became AUC 0.94 vs 0.77 only once measured across all cases. Tag the 6b top 20 plus about 10 random median-error controls with one primary label each. The labels: label-centre error, wrong link, MERGE, split/confluent, tiny, shrunk, huge/irregular, distractor, deformation, appearance change, window edge, or readout (correct peak, bad soft-argmax). Use one figure per case: orthogonal slices through the BL centre, true FU point and predicted point, plus the heatmap. This mirrors the side-by-side, registration-synchronised view the annotators used ([Gatidis et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07466-y)). Assign the tag before looking at the error number. Then compute mean, median and CPM@10 per stratum over all 207 lesions. Report a patient-cluster bootstrap CI, because resampling lesions "treat[s] multiple lesions from the same patient as independent observations" ([Karunanayake et al., Eur Radiol Exp 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13369105/)). Report the mean with and without e2c0be2456, which holds 24% of val lesions.

The tags decide whether a pretrained encoder is worth the effort. If the tail is mostly **distractor** cases (a similar lesion or node in the window, heatmap on it), coarse anatomical context is the documented cure ([SAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.02383v3); [DLT](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9578122)). That favours the 8 mm map and SAM-style embeddings. If the tail is mostly MERGE, label-centre or tiny cases, no encoder will fix them. MERGING lesions share one FU point among several BL lesions. Lesions under 5 mm are at most 2.5 voxels across. Those belong in the report as task-definition limits, not as targets for another model. Running the same review on **train** is also leak-free and finds label errors that cost training signal.

The integration note also contains several numbers that are the researcher's own arithmetic, not measurements. They are flagged as such above: the ~4.3 M truncated-encoder parameter count, the 2–4 / 6–9 / 15–30 GB memory figures, the ~60 mm stage-1 receptive field, the 117 GB cache size, and the 1 mm grid offset. The grid offset is the one most worth trusting. It follows directly from the kernel geometry, and the current stem's centring is documented in `cell_mm()`.

## Conclusion

The real question is not "nnFoundation or not". It is whether Team 2's remaining error comes from features that cannot tell look-alike structures apart, or from targets that no feature can pin down. A val worst-case audit answers that in an afternoon, before any SLURM job, and it decides which pretrained model, if any, is worth integrating. If distractors dominate, the published evidence favours anatomical embeddings (SAM/SAM++) over reconstruction features (MAE/nnFoundation): only the former have been shown to cut tracking error and its variance. nnFoundationCNN remains the cleanest engineering path into SiameseNet. Its value should be judged against a random-init twin, not against 6b alone.

The larger lesson is about measurement. Run 6b's lead over runs 6 and 6c is 0.05 mm, while a patient-clustered CI on 28 val patients is likely around ±1 mm (the researcher's expectation, not yet computed). Any pretrained-encoder gain will be of the same order as these noise bands unless it removes gross misses. So the bootstrap CI and the stratified tail metrics are not reporting extras. They are what makes it possible to tell whether a pretrained encoder helped at all. The test split stays sealed until one final model has been frozen.
