# Train on real offsets and grown lesions first

Team 2's next gains will come from changing what the model is trained on, not how the network is built. Two problems drive the remaining error, and they need different fixes. In the joint pipeline (8.74 mm mean with Team 1's centres, measured with the older run-6b-class model), the tail comes from **starting offsets beyond the ±24 mm the model has ever seen**. The first priority is therefore a training offset sampler built from Team 1's real residuals, plus a wide component covering the full ±56 mm the window allows, with a cheap two-pass re-centring at inference. On Team 2's synthetic-jitter validation (4.44 mm), the offset barely matters (Spearman ρ = −0.05). There, **lesions whose volume grew more than 4× carry 44.5% of the total error**, and most of that error lands *inside* the grown lesion. The second priority is therefore growth-aware training: a radius-gated heatmap target spread over the FU mask, oversampling of grown pairs, and lesion growth/shrink augmentation. Look-alike lesions come third. They are real but smaller: 12 off-lesion misses carry 23% of val error, and half of those are grown lesions too. Distractor-aware sampling fits naturally into the same preprocessing pass as priority 1. Architecture changes (a learned cost-volume head, cross-attention, deeper stages) rank below all of these. Their plausible gains of 0.2–0.5 mm sit below the 0.67 mm that the 207-lesion val split can resolve. Do two things before any training: re-run the joint evaluation with P3-rand and the free 3-run ensemble, and adopt a fixed 25-epoch schedule with patient-level cross-validation, so that the smaller effects become measurable at all.

## The joint 8.74 mm and the validation 4.44 mm fail for different reasons

The two headline numbers come from different error mechanisms, so one fix will not serve both. Team 2's validation places the follow-up (FU) window at the true lesion plus N(0, 8 mm) per axis, clipped at ±24 mm. Online re-jitter is cut from an 88³ `fu_big` cube, so training can never produce a larger offset ([Team2-Model](../docs/wiki/Team2-Model.md)). Team 1's hybrid centres differ in two ways. They are wider, with per-axis SDs of (7.1, 8.2, 11.7) mm, the widest in z. They also have a heavy tail: **13.9% of val lesions start more than 20 mm off, and 11.5% are still more than 20 mm off after Team 2**. Within 5 mm, the share rises from 38.0% to 58.7%, but within 20 mm it rises only from 86.1% to 88.5% ([Joint experiment results](Joint%20experiment%20results.md)). The model fixes lesions that start close and almost none that start far. That is what training on a clipped Gaussian predicts.

The synthetic val tail looks different. The window offset does not predict the error inside the trained range. The 20–30 mm |t| bin averages 5.93 mm, and only the 6 lesions with an axis offset of 20–25 mm, right at the clip, are clearly worse (9.02 mm) ([errors.csv](../runs/3472221/worst/errors.csv), own analysis). The tail is an appearance problem, examined in the next section.

There is one gap to close before anything else. The joint run used a retrained run-6b model (5.58 mm on synthetic val), not P3-rand (4.44 mm). P3-rand beat 6b by **−1.14 mm, 95% patient-clustered CI [−1.85, −0.56]**, on synthetic val. Much of that gain came on small lesions (r < 5 mm: 4.29 → 2.94 mm) and on patient 3988c7f88e, not on far starts ([Experiments](../docs/wiki/Experiments.md), row 16). How much of the gain survives on Team 1's centres is unknown. The joint `predictions.csv` also has not been split by starting offset. Both are cluster-side analyses of an hour or two, and they set the baseline for every recommendation below.

## Grown lesions carry 44.5% of validation error, mostly inside the lesion

The notes disagree about what drives the val tail. The data-side and architecture notes attribute patient e2c0be2456 (50 val lesions, no gain from the two-scale model: 6.74 → 6.75 mm) to look-alike metastases. They propose distractor training and learned map fusion for it. The tail-analysis note attributes it to lesion growth. The project's own per-lesion errors settle most of this. This report re-ran that analysis by joining P3-rand's per-lesion TTA errors with the dataset CSVs ([errors.csv](../runs/3472221/worst/errors.csv); [lesion CSVs](../data/lesion_csv/)).

| Stratum | n | Mean error (TTA) | Share of total val error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volume ratio FU/BL > 4 (grown) | 49 | 8.35 mm | 44.5% |
| All other lesions | 158 | 3.22 mm | 55.5% |
| Grown, outside e2c0be2456 | 11 | 9.60 mm | — |
| e2c0be2456, not grown | 12 | 2.84 mm | — |
| Errors > 10 mm landing *inside* the FU equal-volume radius | 12 | — | 18.4% |
| Errors > 10 mm landing *outside* it (off-lesion misses) | 12 | — | 23.3% |

Growth is the largest single driver, and it is not one patient's quirk. Within e2c0be2456, non-grown lesions average 2.84 mm, as good as the rest of val. Grown lesions elsewhere are just as bad (9.60 mm, n = 11). Growth is not a small-template effect either. Grown lesions with a small BL radius (< 5 mm) average 7.58 mm and those with a larger one 9.37 mm, while small non-grown lesions average 2.86 mm. Of the grown lesions' 44.5% share, **30.8 points fall inside the FU lesion and only 13.7 outside it**. So the dominant failure is not landing on the wrong structure. It is failing to find the centre of a mass that no longer resembles its small BL template. Across all 207 lesions, error beyond the FU radius averages only 0.68 mm. All 12 lesions with r_FU > 20 mm land within one radius of the target.

The look-alike hypothesis is not dead, though. The 12 off-lesion misses carry 23.3% of total error. Six are grown lesions (five in e2c0be2456). The other six are small, non-grown lesions (r_FU 2.6–7.9 mm) in liver, lung and soft tissue, missed by 12–24 mm. Whether those land on another lesion or on background cannot be decided locally. That needs `cog_fu` in mm, and the image headers are on the cluster. The resolution is therefore an ordering, not a dismissal. Growth fixes address roughly twice the error mass of distractor fixes on synthetic val. Distractor fixes matter more in the joint pipeline, where wider offsets put more look-alikes in the window. Two caveats limit this. Growth is confounded with lesion type: 37 of the 49 grown lesions are soft-tissue/skin, most from one patient. And the equal-volume radius is only a proxy for "inside the mask" on irregular shapes.

The merged-lesion question resolves the same way. The model wiki and the architecture note call the merged-lesion target "ambiguous". The data-side note proposes down-weighting the distance term for MERGING pairs to 0.25. But **all 38 merge groups in the dataset share a single `cog_fu` per group**: the target for every member is the merged mass's centre of gravity (re-checked here: 0 of 38 groups have more than one value). The label is consistent, so down-weighting it throws away the only correct supervision. Seven of the 8 val MERGING lesions also grew more than 4×. MERGING (8 lesions, 10.75 mm, 9.4% of error) is best treated as an extreme case of growth: a small BL lesion whose target is the centre of a much larger FU mass. Keep these pairs in training with their current target, and correct the wiki's wording in §1. Literature agrees that merging is an unsolved failure mode. LesionLocator names "closely located or merging lesions" as a remaining challenge ([Rokuss et al., CVPR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20985)). Published pipelines score merges at group level rather than per member ([arXiv 2509.00613](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00613v1)).

## Priority 1: train on the offsets Team 1 actually produces

The single change with the most direct published support is to match the training offset distribution to the upstream stage's real error. In the 2026 clinician-verified tracker trained on the same Longitudinal-CT data, simulating the FU prompt as half GT-near, half "derived from the registered follow-up point (which may fall outside the lesion)" was the largest ablation gain. **DSC rose from 45.8 to 54.0 and lesion detection rate from 66.2 to 76.0** ([arXiv 2605.23118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23118)). Cascade R-CNN shows the general rule: a regressor "performs best for examples of IoU close to the threshold that the detector was trained on". Training each stage on the previous stage's output distribution gave 38.9 AP, against 35.4 for refinement applied only at inference ([Cai & Vasconcelos, CVPR 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.00726)). SiamRPN++ found that Siamese trackers trained with too small a shift range learn a centre bias, with border probabilities collapsing toward zero ([Li et al., CVPR 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.11703)). That is the joint pipeline's symptom. The Deep Lesion Tracker shows that more spatial variety in training buys robustness to a perturbed start. A perturbed template cost DLT 8.49 CPM@10 points, but DLT-Mix, with extra self-supervised pairs, only 3.62 ([Cai et al., CVPR 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.04872)).

The concrete design is a three-part mixture, drawn per sample. About 40% comes from today's N(0, 8 mm), which protects the median. About 40% are bootstrapped Team 1 train residual *vectors*, not per-axis draws, so that the wider z and any axis correlation are kept. The remaining ~20% is uniform over ±56 mm, or a heavy-tailed Student-t clipped there, to cover test-time offsets that Team 1's train residuals miss. The Cascade R-CNN finding argues for keeping the narrow component: a model trained only on far starts loses precision on near ones. Two checks come first. Team 1's hybrid gate (τ = 25 mm) was tuned on val. If any part of Team 1 was fitted on train, its train residuals will be optimistically narrow, so use out-of-fold residuals or compare the train and val histograms before trusting them. And select checkpoints and mixture weights on the joint `hybrid_tta` readout, not on synthetic val, which this change may worsen by a few tenths of a mm.

This also resolves the third disagreement. The architecture note calls a larger FU search window (96³ at 2 mm) "the change most aligned with the joint tail". The data note says the 128 mm window is already big enough. The project's data favours the data note: **100% of Team 1's hybrid val centres already lie within 56 mm of the target on every axis** ([Joint experiment results](Joint%20experiment%20results.md)). The lesion is in the window. The model was never trained to look for it there. The binding constraint is the 88³ `fu_big` cube, not the 64³ window. Re-cutting train files with `--jitter-max 56` gives a 120³ cube (about 3.5 MB per file, against about 1.4 MB now). The model needs no change, since its soft-argmax already spans ±62 mm. A 96³ window would cost about 3.4× compute (≈ 12 h per 50 epochs) for a coverage gain that the hybrid centres do not need. It only becomes relevant if the pipeline must accept registration-propagated centres, 10.6% of which fall outside the window.

Two inference-side complements cost no retraining. Two-pass re-centring runs the model, re-cuts the window at the prediction from the raw FU scan (the joint pipeline already cuts from centres), and runs again. This is DaSiamRPN's local-to-global step, where "one step iteration … is sufficient" ([Zhu et al., ECCV 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.06048)). It brings a 30–40 mm start back into the trained range whenever the first pass lands near the right lesion. Gate the second pass on confidence. On P3-rand val, **the spread of the three two-scale runs flags errors over 10 mm with AUROC 0.92, against 0.84 for 1 − peak probability** (in-sample, so optimistic). That ranking matches medical-segmentation evidence that ensemble agreement is the best simple failure detector ([Zenk et al., MedIA 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841524003177)). The same three checkpoints give the cheapest measured gain in the project: averaging their plain predictions gives **4.27 mm against 4.57 mm for P3-rand alone**, and 14.47 against 17.08 mm on the top 20 ([Team2-Model](../docs/wiki/Team2-Model.md); tail analysis). Do not use a zero-offset fallback when confidence is low. On synthetic val it worsens the mean at every threshold tested (4.97 mm at τ = 0.2), because the model beats the window centre even on most low-confidence lesions.

The expected effect is a back-of-envelope estimate. About 24 of 208 joint val lesions are still more than 20 mm off. If half were found at the model's typical 4–5 mm instead of about 25–30 mm, the joint mean would fall by about 12 × 22 / 208 ≈ 1.3 mm, to roughly 7.4 mm. Look-alikes in the wider windows will cap this.

## Priorities 2 and 3: teach the model grown lesions and their neighbours

For grown lesions, the model's own readout points to the fix. A soft-argmax over a distribution spread uniformly over the FU mask returns exactly the mask centre of gravity, which is the target. The current mask term, −log Σ p inside the mask, is satisfied by a single peak anywhere in the lesion. A template match cannot place that peak at the centre of a mass 5–1000× larger than its BL template. Replace it, only for r_FU above about 10 mm, with a cross-entropy against a uniform distribution over the max-pooled FU mask. The network then only has to "paint the lesion". The radius gate matters: run 6's wide Gaussian heatmap target blurred the map and cost about 1 mm of median (row 6 of [Experiments](../docs/wiki/Experiments.md)), so small lesions must keep their sharp peak. The two companions are nearly free. First, oversample or up-weight (×2–3) the roughly 19% of train pairs with volume ratio > 4 and the 6.8% that are MERGING (own count from the CSVs; val has 23.7% grown). Second, add LesionLocator-style growth/shrink deformations to the FU patch and mask only, recomputing the centre from the warped mask. On a Tübingen melanoma longitudinal set, LesionLocator's synthetic follow-ups cut centre error **from 5.09 to 3.89 mm** ([Rokuss et al., CVPR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20985)). That gain mixes extra data volume with the deformation type, so it is an upper-bound analogue, not a forecast. The ceiling is well defined: bringing grown lesions to the non-grown mean would save 49 × (8.35 − 3.22) / 207 ≈ 1.2 mm on synthetic val. A realistic third of that, about 0.4 mm, is this report's estimate. Judge it on the grown stratum, where the effect is concentrated, not only on the overall mean.

For look-alikes, Longitudinal-CT's exhaustive segmentation gives an advantage the published trackers lacked: every other lesion in the FU window is a known distractor ([Gatidis et al., Sci Data 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07466-y)). DaSiamRPN showed that training mostly on background yields "lesion vs background" features rather than instance-level ones. Its semantic negative pairs raised VOT2016 EAO from 0.368 to 0.389 ([Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.06048)). Removing hard-negative sampling from the SAM anatomical embedding cut lesion-matching accuracy from 91.1% to 89.0%. Using only the hardest negatives inflated maximum errors, so mix them in, do not replace ordinary samples ([Yan et al., TMI 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.02383v3)). Two changes follow. For about 20% of samples, centre the window on, or between, a neighbouring FU lesion within about 40 mm. Then add a small penalty on softmax mass that falls on other lesions' masks, including NEWLYAPPEARING ones. Both need the full FU instance map and the larger `fu_big`. Do this in the same preprocessing re-run as priority 1, so it costs one data pass, not two. Expected gain on synthetic val is 0.1–0.5 mm (estimate). It should be larger on the joint pipeline, where the distractor-centred windows mimic the far starts. Two cheaper checks should come first. Compute the swap statistic on the cluster: for each off-lesion miss, does the prediction fall within r_FU of another lesion's `cog_fu`? And skip assignment machinery such as Hungarian exclusivity over heatmap peaks unless swaps prove common.

A third data idea is a medium-cost bet. Single-scan self-pairs take the BL and FU patch from the same scan, with independent augmentation, a sampled offset and the growth field. That turns the 1,969 dropped DISAPPEARING, NEWLYAPPEARING and unclear rows into extra training pairs. DLT's evidence is mixed: self-pairs alone were worse than real pairs (8.8 vs 7.0 mm MED), but mixing them in bought robustness to perturbed starts ([Cai et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.04872)). Mix them at 20–30% of each batch, use train-split patients only, and try it after priorities 1–3.

## Architecture changes rank below data, and validation cannot see them yet

The architecture options are sound but unproven at this data size, and their expected gains are below what val can resolve. The paired CI between P3 and P3-rand implies a standard error of about 0.24 mm, so the **minimum detectable difference on val is about 0.67 mm** at 80% power. Seed noise (about 0.1 mm, from a single pair of runs) is minor next to patient sampling. Data sampling is the dominant variance source in benchmarks generally ([Bouthillier et al., MLSys 2021](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2021/file/0184b0cd3cfb185989f858a1d9f5c1eb-Paper.pdf)). Val also picks the epoch: P3-rand's best was epoch 18 of 50, and val rises after epochs 20–30. Two process changes therefore come before any architecture work. Fix the schedule a priori at 20–25 epochs and take the last EMA weights, which also halves run time to about 1.8 h. Then compare finalists by 5-fold patient-level cross-validation over the 255 train+val patients, which cuts the detectable difference to about 0.2–0.25 mm at about 9 GPU-hours per configuration. Without these two changes, most of the table below is unmeasurable.

Among architecture changes, the best-supported cheap one is a small learned 3D CNN over the stacked similarity maps, replacing the fixed sum. It should be initialised to reproduce today's weighted sum. In GMFlow, the first four conv blocks over a cost volume cut EPE from 18.83 to 10.99 ([GMFlow, arXiv 2111.13680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13680)). In 3D CT registration, cost-volume processing matters mainly for large displacements ([RWCNet, arXiv 2302.02283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02283)), the joint regime. A CNN can learn "trust a fine peak only if the coarse map agrees", which targets off-lesion misses. A mask- or Gaussian-gated template pairs naturally with it. TLT's template restriction gave its largest single ablation gain (7.58 → 6.76 mm) ([Tang et al., MICCAI 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06252)). Its value here is smaller, because centring and mask pooling already restrict the template. Cross-attention on the 16³ grid is the bigger, riskier step: transformers are data-hungry, and 2,159 pairs is small. A stage-3 (16 mm) map promises less than the 8 mm map's 1.1 mm, because the 128 mm window caps the context it can add. Readout tweaks are nearly exhausted: a per-lesion oracle choosing between TTA and TTA-local reaches only 4.19 mm. Pretrained encoders, intensity augmentation (single-vendor, portal-venous, one kernel class; [Gatidis et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07466-y)), patient reweighting and external datasets are low priority for this metric.

## Conclusion

The project's own error tables changed the picture more than the literature did. The architecture discussion assumed a look-alike problem in a crowded patient. The data show mainly a centring problem on lesions that grew, plus an offset range the training sampler never covers. Both are training-data problems, so the cheapest fixes are also the best-supported ones. One preprocessing pass (a 120³ cube, the full FU instance map, the volume ratio and topology stored per file) enables priorities 1 to 3 together. Each can then be switched on as a sampler or loss flag and measured separately on the joint readout.

The deeper lesson is about measurement. Team 2's synthetic validation has done its job: it took the model from 12.47 to 4.44 mm. It now steers toward the wrong target, because it rewards precision near the window centre while the real pipeline fails at the edges. From here on, the joint `hybrid_tta` number should be the selection metric, cross-validation the comparison tool, and the grown and off-lesion strata the diagnostics. The test split should stay closed until one configuration is frozen. The open empirical question is the share of off-lesion misses that are swaps onto other lesions. It decides whether priority 3 is the next big lever or a minor one, and one cluster query with the image headers answers it.
